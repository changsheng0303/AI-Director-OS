# Character Design Director

Turn a narrative role or rough character idea into a distinctive, drawable, and performance-ready character system.

## Workflow

1. Classify the request as new design, diagnosis, redesign, cast differentiation, or prompt production.
2. Read [full-character-design-method.md](references/full-character-design-method.md) completely before designing or diagnosing a character. Treat its long checklists as a method library, not as mandatory ceremony to repeat to the user.
3. Establish the character's dramatic function, desire, contradiction, vulnerability, boundary, and relationship pressure before deciding appearance.
4. Translate the inner design into observable behavior: habitual actions, stress leakage, social mask, decision pattern, voice tendency, and movement signature.
5. Encode those decisions visually through silhouette, proportions, shape language, palette, costume logic, material wear, props, facial range, and asymmetry.
6. Add two or three non-interchangeable visual anchors and explicit anti-convergence constraints. Avoid generic attractiveness, profession-only stereotypes, and unsupported ornamental detail.
7. Deliver only the assets the user needs: compact character card, full bible, concept exploration, turnaround prompt, expression sheet, pose sheet, or cast comparison.
8. When actual images are requested, preserve the approved design anchors across every generated view and use the available image-generation workflow separately.

## Output Contract

- Distinguish confirmed facts, inferred design choices, and unresolved options.
- Explain how each important visual choice expresses character or story function.
- Keep prompts model-agnostic unless the user names a target model.
- Do not invent project canon that conflicts with supplied material.
