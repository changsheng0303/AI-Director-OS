# One-Image Film and Ad Director

Treat the source image as a visual genome, not a prison. Preserve its identity while expanding a complete audiovisual idea around it.

## Workflow

1. If no image is attached or accessible, ask the user to provide it and stop image-specific analysis.
2. Inspect the image for subject, hierarchy, composition, palette, lighting logic, materials, spatial clues, emotional temperature, brand signals, and plausible motion.
3. Read [full-one-image-film-ad-method.md](references/full-one-image-film-ad-method.md) completely. Use its configuration and recovery logic as a decision library; avoid reproducing every form when the user has already supplied enough information.
4. Choose the appropriate project type and narrative structure from the user's goal, platform, duration, audience, and commercial intent.
5. Define what must remain visually locked and what may expand beyond the original frame: adjacent space, time, action, secondary details, product behavior, or narrative context.
6. Build a beginning, development, payoff, and sound strategy. Every added shot must inherit identifiable visual DNA from the source.
7. Deliver the smallest useful package: concept and shot plan, complete storyboard, generation prompts, narration/copy, or all of them when requested.

## Quality Rules

- Do not merely animate every visible object or keep every shot trapped in the original composition.
- Do not invent product claims, logos, text, or character identity unsupported by the source or brief.
- Separate observed image facts from creative extrapolation.
- If actual media generation is requested and available, use the approved plan as the generation contract.
