# Genre Playbooks

## General Rule

For every genre, define core emotion, protagonist desire, source of obstacle, and ending payoff. Genre elements must serve character choice, not replace it.

## Sweet Romance

- Rules: relationship progress needs misunderstanding, distance, status gap, or values gap as pressure.
- Payoffs: protection, preference, mutual pursuit, crisis support, restrained confession, missed-chance repair.
- Avoid: coercion framed as love, stalking, forced intimacy, objectifying the heroine.
- Alternatives: replace coercion with trust earned through respected boundaries; replace rescue fantasy with mutual rescue.

## Comeback / Underdog

- Rules: oppression is clear; comeback depends on ability, information, choice, or cost.
- Payoffs: public proof, reversal, identity reveal, old-debt settlement, stage victory.
- Avoid: thoughtless humiliation, pure cheat-code power, poverty/career/gender mockery.
- Alternatives: use evidence, craft, teamwork, or professional competence instead of a sudden savior.

## Suspense

- Rules: every scene advances one question or clue; clues must be fair on rewatch.
- Points: liar, evidence gap, timeline mismatch, identity swap, motive reversal.
- Avoid: arbitrary culprit, evidence from nowhere, sudden protagonist stupidity.
- Alternatives: use a character secret to mislead, then reinterpret the same evidence later.

## Revenge

- Rules: harm, target, evidence chain, and moral cost must be clear.
- Payoffs: patient setup, counterattack, opponent self-destruction, public truth, emotional cost.
- Avoid: glorified private violence, gore, criminal tutorial, indiscriminate retaliation.
- Alternatives: use legal route, public evidence, business pressure, or social accountability.

## Family

- Rules: conflict comes from love, duty, debt, control, generation gap, or unspoken sacrifice.
- Payoffs: hidden care, years-late truth, parent/child choice, role reversal.
- Avoid: forced reunion, moral blackmail, beautified abuse.
- Alternatives: use dinner conflict, old objects, school, hospital, housing, or inheritance pressure.

## Workplace

- Rules: role, target, resource, deadline, authority, and responsibility must be specific.
- Payoffs: professional proof, project recovery, exposed blame-shifting, team win.
- Avoid: industry nonsense, workplace abuse as growth, romance solving career problems.
- Alternatives: use meeting confrontation, data evidence, client acceptance, or competitor crisis.

## Social Issue

- Rules: show the issue through a specific person's situation; do not write only slogans.
- Points: small-person pressure, institutional gap, bias, silence cost, hard choice.
- Avoid: labeling groups, consuming suffering, unverified claims, hate incitement.
- Alternatives: use one concrete case and let the protagonist choose under pressure.

## Comedy

- Rules: comedy comes from desire, misunderstanding, identity mismatch, contrast, and timing.
- Points: miscommunication, excessive seriousness, small goal going out of control, repeated escalation.
- Avoid: discriminatory jokes, humiliation of the vulnerable, jokes that do not move plot.
- Alternatives: make the powerful lose control; turn verbal jokes into action reversals.

## Horror

- Rules: establish daily order, then break the rule. Fear comes from unknown, taboo, approach, and uncertainty.
- Points: spatial abnormality, repeated sound, rule failure, familiar person becoming strange.
- Avoid: gore-only shock, superstition promotion, dangerous imitation, minors as spectacle.
- Alternatives: use psychological fear, constrained space, sound, and object changes.

## Costume / Historical Fantasy

- Rules: define etiquette, hierarchy, power structure, and identity rules first.
- Points: identity reveal, court/family struggle, marriage reversal, plot within plot, old case reopened.
- Avoid: historical confusion, modern slogans in dialogue, distorted ethnic history, unexamined superstition.
- Alternatives: use fictionalization, etiquette, edict, marriage contract, clan rule, exam, or military order as pressure.
