# Film Concept Routing

Use this reference when the request is specifically a film synopsis, concept anchor, or early story direction and the intended creative mode materially changes the structure. If the mode is already evident, proceed without presenting a menu. Ask one compact choice only when the difference would change the story's core architecture.

## Shared concept anchor

Before expanding scenes, establish only what the chosen mode needs:

- logline: protagonist, situation, conflict, action, and cost;
- controlling idea or authorial intent;
- target form and approximate duration;
- protagonist's visible desire, underlying need, flaw, and arc direction;
- opposing force and its mirror relationship to the protagonist;
- inciting disruption, crisis choice, climax action, resolution state;
- thematic distinction and any setup/payoff objects that truly matter.

Do not invent downstream assets, camera plans, or image prompts at concept stage.

## Classical long-form or conventional series

Use a causal dramatic spine. Lock the inciting disruption early enough to destabilize the protagonist's ordinary world, escalate the cost through the middle, and end in a crisis between incompatible values. The climax is the physical action that answers that crisis; the resolution shows the new world state.

Track setup/payoff objects as story mechanisms, not decorative props. Every important object should have a setup location, payoff location, and meaning.

## Festival or art-film short

Build three connected layers:

1. **Story:** the observable condition or event in a specific physical space.
2. **Metaphor:** one recurring physical object, natural phenomenon, or action that carries the inner or social pressure.
3. **Intent:** aesthetic aim, historical/social context, concept, standpoint, recurring gesture, and motif.

The character anchor may be a protagonist plus a mirror figure or an unreachable/unspeaking object of address. Do not force a conventional action climax when the intended culmination is a physicalized psychological break, refusal, compromise, or unresolved image.

## Streaming or hook-driven short drama

Define:

- the opening condition of loss, humiliation, danger, or impossible pressure;
- the credible counterforce or hidden leverage;
- the logic connecting multiple genre engines;
- a reversal chain: pressure, reveal, counterattack, consequence;
- an early visual hook and a final unresolved threat or promise.

Do not reduce every project to a power fantasy. The hidden leverage, reversal, and cliffhanger must arise from the confirmed premise and character cost.

## User-defined mode

Preserve the user's requested base architecture and change only the named dimensions. Record which classical, art-film, or hook-driven assumptions remain active and which have been replaced.

## Output boundary

Return a concise synopsis or concept contract marked as proposal until approved. Do not automatically create a character bible, world bible, treatment, screenplay, asset prompt, or video prompt.
