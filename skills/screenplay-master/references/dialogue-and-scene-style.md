# Dialogue and Scene Style

## Dialogue Compression

- Keep only four functions: create conflict, reveal relationship, push choice, complete reversal.
- Remove explanatory lines; turn background into action, prop, expression, or challenge.
- One line should carry one information point.
- Prefer short, oral, performable sentences.
- Keep at most one punchline per scene.
- Ask during review: if this line is deleted, does conflict, information, or emotion suffer? If not, delete it.

## Commercial Dialogue

- Product or brand language must be dramatized.
- The product should solve conflict, create misunderstanding, reveal evidence, or complete reversal.
- Do not stack selling points unless the user explicitly asks for ad-copy variants.

## Visual Cues

Write cues for production, not literary description.

Template:

`Image: who is where doing what; Action: visible behavior; Emotion: change point; Sound/Subtitle: necessary information.`

Rules:

- For vertical video, prioritize close-up, medium close-up, and key prop shots.
- The opening 0-5% must show abnormal image, conflict line, abnormal result, or strong question.
- Use evidence shot, expression pause, prop reveal, or blocking change for reversals.
- Sound cues should be executable: phone ring, door knock, silence, ambient sound, emphasized pause, subtitle emphasis.

## Shootability

- Keep characters few, locations few, actions visible.
- Prefer low-cost locations: living room, office, hallway, elevator lobby, store, car, downstairs entrance.
- Use hospital, school, court, police, military, and similar settings cautiously because they can raise production and compliance complexity.
- Every scene needs a visible action, not only people explaining.
- Every scene should serve hook, escalation, misunderstanding, evidence, reversal, emotional payoff, CTA, or cliffhanger.
