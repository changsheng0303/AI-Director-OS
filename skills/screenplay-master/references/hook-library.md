# Hook, Cliffhanger, Reversal, and Title Library

## Opening Hooks

| Type | Use |
|---|---|
| Abnormal image | Start with danger, embarrassment, loss of control, impossible contrast, or a strong visual. |
| Conflict line | Use one confrontational line to expose relationship and pressure. |
| Counterintuitive question | Make the viewer ask why this is happening. |
| Result first | Show ending, crisis, or iconic moment before the cause. |
| Pain/desire hit | Expose shame, loss, desire, or urgent need immediately. |

## Cliffhangers

| Type | Use |
|---|---|
| Episode problem | Every episode has a clear question to resolve. |
| Next-episode pull | End with new evidence, threat, choice, or relationship shift. |
| Setup/payoff | Plant information and later pay it off; do not only stack suspense. |
| Stage payoff | Deliver real progress every 3-5 episodes. |
| Emotional residue | Leave shock, regret, anger, tenderness, or satisfaction. |

## Reversals

| Type | Use |
|---|---|
| Relationship reversal | Enemy becomes ally, family becomes obstacle, lover hides a secret. |
| Misunderstanding reversal | Audience and character believe A, then learn B. |
| Evidence reversal | New evidence changes the meaning of a prior scene. |
| Value reversal | A weak person, object, or product becomes decisive in conflict. |
| Choice reversal | A character makes an irreversible decision that changes the story direction. |

## Title Directions

- Conflict: confrontation, humiliation, betrayal, reversal, showdown.
- Emotion: satisfaction, tears, laughter, sympathy, anger.
- Suspense: secret, truth, evidence, identity, abnormal result.
- Reversal: "It turns out...", "No one expected...", "The real..."
- Iconic moment: extract from punchline, climax action, or shareable clip.

## Prohibitions

- Do not begin by explaining background, world, resume, or product features.
- Do not use delay as cliffhanger without stage payoff.
- Do not break character motive for a twist.
- Do not use unprovable claims, fake scarcity, fake authority, or fake user cases in commercial titles.
- Do not copy distinctive plots, lines, character settings, or set pieces from existing works.
