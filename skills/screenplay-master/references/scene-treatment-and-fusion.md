# Scene Treatment and Screenplay Fusion

Use the treatment section when converting an approved concept and character foundation into a scene-by-scene physical plan. Use the fusion section when assembling approved synopsis, character, world, and treatment materials into a director-ready screenplay or revising an existing screenplay.

## Treatment: physical scene plan

Write what happens physically. Do not draft full dialogue in the treatment.

Derive scene count from target duration, event density, genre, and intended rhythm. A recommendation may be offered, but do not stop for confirmation when the user has delegated the decision or the scope is already clear.

For each scene record:

- interior/exterior, location, and time;
- causal reason it follows the previous scene;
- characters present and their immediate objective;
- observable action and conflict;
- value change from entry to exit;
- required setup/payoff, world-rule consequence, or information shift;
- whether removing the scene would change the story.

Audit each scene for causality, value movement, Canon conflict, pacing, exposition dumping, and repetition. Repair or remove a scene that has no causal or value function.

### Mode-sensitive rhythm

- **Classical:** setup, inciting disruption, escalating confrontation, midpoint change, crisis, climax, resolution.
- **Art-film:** establish condition/context, surface the motif and habitual gesture, let metaphor interact with physical reality, culminate in a visible rupture/refusal/compromise, close on an interpretable image.
- **Hook-driven:** opening pressure, leverage activation, expectation reversal, visible consequence, stronger next threat or promise.
- **Custom:** follow the approved user structure and document deviations from the base mode.

## Screenplay fusion

Before drafting, inventory the actual authority sources:

- approved story or existing screenplay;
- approved character identity and voice;
- relevant world rules, costs, and knowledge asymmetries;
- approved treatment events and scene order;
- exact dialogue that must remain unchanged.

Separate two operations:

1. **Assembly-only:** format and integrate the materials without adding plot actions or expanding dialogue.
2. **Authorized dialogue expansion:** enrich dialogue density and style while preserving scene events, character knowledge, and outcome. Route dialogue authority through `universal-dialogue-core` when voice or hidden information is material.

Do not add an action, prop, event, or line merely to reach a target length. Worldbuilding should appear through physical consequences, behavior, objects, and information gaps rather than explanatory speeches.

When the source has few scenes but the requested form implies a much longer work, identify whether the material is a complete short work or one batch of a larger work. Do not solve the mismatch by padding.

## Director-ready format

Use a consistent scene heading and observable action. Identify a character's first appearance, keep performance notes brief and playable, and preserve exact approved dialogue text. Store changes as a new version and mark only affected downstream outputs as possibly stale.
