# Single-shot Physical Control

Use this reference when a Seedance shot is likely to fail because the model may choose the wrong first frame, blocking, eyeline, landmark distance, prop state, motion physics, lens behavior, or light direction. Apply only the controls that protect the current shot.

## Isolate the shot

Keep only characters, references, props, locations, dialogue, and sounds that are visible or audible in this shot. Remove previous-scene summaries, stale reference labels, scene numbers, and unused subjects.

Treat reference roles separately:

- identity reference controls face, body proportions, stable marks, and approved clothing;
- location reference controls geography, architecture, materials, landmarks, atmosphere, and motivated light—not camera angle unless requested;
- prop/vehicle reference controls form, scale, material, contact, damage, doors, and operating state;
- style reference cannot override identity, blocking, action, optics, or light direction.

Never invent a reference label or carry an unused label from a previous shot.

## First frame and blocking

- State whether required subjects are already visible in the first frame. Do not permit an empty establishing opening unless requested.
- Give each important subject a screen position, world position, depth layer, body orientation, eyeline, and movement direction.
- Replace vague proximity with measurable contact or distance: within one metre, back against the wall, hand on the handle, feet inside the marked area.
- Separate torso direction from eye direction when the relationship matters.
- Define which camera side preserves the intended screen direction and axis.

## Camera and optics

Choose one optical behavior that matches the content: natural perspective, immersive wide spatial coverage, portrait compression, or distant observation. Describe the visible result and camera-to-subject distance. Do not mix portrait isolation, vast geography, and macro detail in one uninterrupted shot; use an approved cut when the content category truly changes.

Camera movement must describe a physical operation and purpose. Avoid simultaneous compound moves that make blocking unreadable.

Prefer one continuous take unless the user or approved Shot Table requires a cut, insert, reverse, montage, or a second content category that cannot be staged clearly in one view. For a controlled multi-shot sequence, define each shot's first-frame subjects, camera position, blocking, action, cut type, and end state. Preserve axis, screen direction, eyelines, light direction, prop/hand state, damage, particles, and emotional progression across every cut.

When the target engine responds to field-of-view language, an optional diagonal FOV can reinforce the chosen optical behavior. Use it only as support for observable results and camera distance, not as unverified metadata. Never force incompatible portrait compression, vast geography, and macro detail into one optical state.

## Physical causality

Specify only relevant properties:

- mass, inertia, acceleration and deceleration;
- foot contact, weight transfer, friction and ground pressure;
- hand-to-prop contact and which hand holds it;
- fabric and hair lag;
- liquid adhesion, dripping and gravity;
- particle wind direction and accumulation;
- continuity of wounds, dirt, snow, fire, smoke, doors, vehicles, and object states.

An action should have a readable cause, contact, result, and residual state. Do not allow teleporting props, frictionless feet, weightless weapons, or a reset after a cut.

## Light direction

Lock the source, direction, camera side, exposed area, shadow side, background level, and exposure priority. If the intention is silhouette or backlight, keep the camera on the shadow side and do not add unmotivated frontal beauty light.

## Timing and dialogue

Divide time by the real completion time of confirmed actions, spoken lines, reactions, camera moves, and cuts. Segments may be unequal but must be continuous and cover the selected clip duration. Do not invent filler actions to occupy time.

Only the approved speaker moves their lips during a line. Preserve exact dialogue and define whether other characters listen, interrupt, or remain silent.

Previous dialogue used only for emotional continuity is audio context, not permission to visualize the people or objects it mentions. Keep on-screen content limited to the active shot.

## Prompt density and constraints

Use high detail for identity anchors, first frame, blocking, eyelines, landmark distance, hand/prop state, timed action, optical behavior, light direction, physics, and exact dialogue. Keep generic beauty language, inactive background detail, and already-clear reference description brief.

State the correct positive condition first. Add a local negative lock only for a likely failure, immediately beside the protected rule. Avoid a generic wall of negative terms.

## Final check

Confirm: active references only; correct first frame; clear blocking and eyelines; correct prop hand/state; physically possible timing; stable screen and light direction; no unapproved character, action, dialogue, or cut.
