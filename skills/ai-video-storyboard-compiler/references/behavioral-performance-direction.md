# Behavioral Performance Direction

Use this reference for shots where acting quality materially carries the scene: dialogue, confrontation, concealment, reaction, close-up, status reversal, or emotionally loaded silence. Skip it for ordinary geography, inserts, and purely mechanical action.

## Core model

Performance is behavior under pressure, not an emotion label. For every active character, identify only what the shot needs:

- **Goal:** what the character is trying to make another person do, believe, reveal, or stop.
- **Obstacle and stakes:** what blocks the goal and what failure costs now.
- **Tactic:** the current playable action, such as pressure, charm, evade, bargain, shame, provoke, or delay.
- **Beat change:** the visible moment when new information, tactic failure, or a power shift changes behavior.
- **Subtext:** the difference between the spoken line and the pursued goal.

Do not write “sad,” “angry,” or “nervous” as the performance instruction. Translate the state into observable behavior.

## Reusable performance identity

For a recurring main character, the project may keep a compact performance profile separate from appearance and current scene state. Include only durable, playable traits:

- physical history expressed through weight, posture, centre of gravity, tempo, and gait;
- stable voice identity: age impression, register, accent/background, sentence length, rhythm, and how pressure changes delivery;
- signature habits with explicit triggers;
- a masking behavior and the precise condition that breaks the mask;
- purposeful eye behavior: tracking, blink quality, focus changes, and eyes leading thought before head or speech;
- one contradictory or vulnerable response when it is genuinely supported by Canon.

Do not put clothing, camera, lighting, or color in the performance profile. A scene adapts the profile rather than copying it verbatim. If a usual behavior is physically impossible in the current pose, preserve its underlying energy through another outlet instead of deleting the character's behavioral logic.

Voice identity remains stable across scenes. Scene direction may change pace, volume, interruption, breath, or vocal break, but should not silently replace the character's established voice.

## Observable direction

Prefer a small number of playable signals:

- listening begins before the other line fully ends;
- thought or evaluation precedes the reply;
- breath, gaze, posture, distance, voice level, or hand activity changes at a beat;
- a practical physical task continues under dialogue and may stop once as emphasis;
- eyes track people or objects with purposeful micro-movement rather than a fixed stare;
- high status tends toward stillness and deliberate timing; low status tends toward self-touch, permission-seeking gaze, fragmented speech, or excess motion;
- group reactions propagate asynchronously rather than in perfect sync.

Use distance changes only when motivated. Closing distance may pressure or seek intimacy; withdrawal may refuse, hide, or concede. Preserve the scene geography and axis already locked by the storyboard.

Pauses are events, not empty duration. A pause should contain evaluation, decision, refusal, listening, or a physical task. In close-up, reduce behavior: a gaze decision, breath change, or interrupted task usually reads more truthfully than multiple facial instructions.

## Continuity

- Carry physical and emotional residue into the next shot; do not reset after a major event.
- Preserve stable voice identity and recurring behavioral anchors, adapting their physical outlet to the current pose and setting.
- Present the character in an active state the model can render. Avoid long chains describing how the state is reached when a clear action phase is available.

## Shot Table use

When useful, attach a compact `performance_direction` to the shot containing:

```text
goal; obstacle; current tactic; listening/reaction cue; visible beat change; physical task or distance change
```

Omit empty fields. Never change Canon dialogue, invent a new action outcome, redesign appearance, or add camera and color instructions to this performance field.

## Quality gate

Reject or revise performance direction when it shows any of these patterns:

- facial expression merely illustrates the dialogue;
- the character performs the final emotion before the triggering information arrives;
- listeners remain neutral until it is their turn to speak;
- one tactic continues after it visibly fails;
- every group member reacts at the same instant and intensity;
- a major event leaves no physical or vocal residue in the next beat;
- threatening action is announced by generic villain gestures rather than arising from the situation;
- the instruction contains only emotion adjectives and no playable behavior.

A strong close-up contains a goal, listening, at least one visible evaluation or tactic shift, physical continuity, and subtext or contradiction. Use fewer signals when fewer are enough.
