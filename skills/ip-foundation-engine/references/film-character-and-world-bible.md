# Film Character and World Bible

Use this reference for an approved film concept when durable character identity, voice, relationships, or world constraints are needed before treatment and screenplay work. Skip full expansion for minor roles and realistic worlds whose ordinary rules need no special definition.

## Character bible

Bind every character choice to the approved concept mode and story function. For each approved main character, capture only durable facts:

- age range, role, occupation/status, origin when relevant;
- first-read physical impression, durable marks, and a costume progression rule when story-relevant;
- flaw, visible desire, underlying need, fear, self-directed grievance, other-directed grievance;
- concise formative history;
- arc start, destabilizing change, and possible end state;
- voice identity: line length, register, slang, self-justification pattern, silence conditions, recurring phrasing;
- directed relationships, including warmth, distance, conflict, and asymmetric forms of address;
- one or more story-functional signature objects.

Test voice separation by removing speaker labels from representative lines. If two characters remain interchangeable, revise their objectives, register, rhythm, defense pattern, or knowledge—not merely their catchphrases.

### Mode emphasis

- **Classical:** desire/need/flaw, opposing mirror, arc, voice, directed relationships, setup/payoff objects.
- **Art-film:** the body carries the film's intent; emphasize habitual gesture, psychic wound, ideological pressure, silence, mirror figure, or unreachable addressee.
- **Hook-driven:** emphasize the credible contrast between surface identity and hidden leverage, the trigger that breaks restraint, the visible reversal behavior, and the pressure network. Avoid disposable caricatures unless the approved tone deliberately requires them.
- **Custom:** inherit only the approved dimensions from the selected base mode.

Character identity is durable Canon. Current costume damage, mood, location, knowledge gained in a scene, and immediate behavior belong in project runtime state.

## Optional world bible

Create this only when the world differs materially from ordinary reality or when production needs explicit rules.

Record:

- what differs from reality and what remains ordinary;
- technology, magic, institutions, or supernatural systems;
- what the system can do, cannot do, and costs to use;
- who has access and who does not;
- only the historical events that shape the current story;
- current political, social, environmental, or technological conditions;
- factions, goals, methods, leaders, and conflict points;
- story-functional locations;
- artifacts with setup, payoff, and meaning;
- knowledge hierarchy: what each character knows, does not know, and falsely believes.

Expose world rules through action, objects, consequences, and ordinary character reactions. Avoid dialogue whose only function is to explain a rule everyone in the world already knows.

## Boundary

Do not rewrite the approved synopsis, treatment, or screenplay. New ideas remain proposals until accepted. Return only durable character/world truth to Foundation-lite or the full Foundation package selected by the user.
